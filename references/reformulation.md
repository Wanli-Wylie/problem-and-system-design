## How to use these moves

A formulation is revised when evidence shows that the difficulty at hand is the formulation's. That is not the correction of an error. The earlier formulation was the representation the work needed at the time, and what it produced is kept as material; in the source history, the owner closed a large, self-consistent problem model by saying "[The earlier model] itself did not fail ... That value should be preserved" (the owner of the source history, 2026-09-25; `cases.md` P10), and changed its standing from specification to map without rewriting a line of it.

Each move is a question with a consequence. Reach for the one the situation raises; the moves are not a sequence. The literature's worked reformulations are mostly told after the fact, as a path from a worse formulation to a better one with no dead ends, and treating such a path as a procedure is an error the moves are written to avoid: what they offer is a set of readings, operations and signs that apply in the middle of a difficulty, when several diagnoses are possible and one is wrong.

M1 and M2 carry worked applications to ordinary systems outside the domain in which the moves were learned; they are examples, not answers. Examples from the source history are marked as such, with their kind (enduring, contextual or objective-bound), and told in full in [cases.md](cases.md). A quotation from that history replaces the project's name in brackets and is located as "(the owner of the source history, <date>; `cases.md` P<n>)" or "(agents' text of the source history; `cases.md` P<n>)". Where a move has a twin among the principles and judgment moves, the twin is linked, not restated.

## M1 The three readings

**Question.** Is this difficulty a missing capability, a wrong design, or a formulation that manufactures it; and if the formulation, at which level: the premise of the question being asked, or the premise of the problem the system is asked to solve?

**Consequence.** Answer before repairing. The readings are taken in one order: the world first, since whether something that already exists answers the difficulty is a finding of its own ([formulation.md](formulation.md), F4); then the three places to intervene, read together: the solver's capability, its structure, the statement of the problem. Choose by which change is cheapest and least damaging to what else depends on the thing changed. Where the formulation manufactured the difficulty, intervening there is cheapest once the change is made, because the mechanisms that served it fall away together. In a live system whose parts move one at a time, the cost of the transition is stated separately: it can exceed the repairs it replaces, and [C4 Expand the platform, not the special case](principles.md#c4-expand-the-platform-not-the-special-case) gives the form of a migration while the old formulation drains. A capability reading leads to widening the foundation. A design reading goes to the design move that acts on it: [J2 The boundary test](judgment.md#j2-the-boundary-test) for an entity that need not exist, [J3 The owner test](judgment.md#j3-the-owner-test) for a second owner of one fact, [B5 One path for loss](principles.md#b5-one-path-for-loss) for a second path for loss, [J12 The premise lever](judgment.md#j12-the-premise-lever) for a premise several mechanisms share. A formulation reading leads to an operation (M2) with a record (M4). The diagnostic that separates the third reading from the first is a counterfactual: if a stronger capability would not change the judgment, the difficulty is not one of capability.

**Why.** A difficulty is not a property of a problem alone. The literature agrees on this across disciplines: for Simon, whether a problem is well structured is "a relation between characteristics of a problem domain ... and the characteristics and power of an implicit or explicit problem solving mechanism"; Amarel measures difficulty as the search a representation leaves to a method; Williams says a formulation's cost must be weighed with "the solution strategy to be employed". So the three readings are not three causes that partition the world. They are three places where one could act, and the question is which act is cheapest and does least harm. The default reading of difficulty is the first ("the implementation is not done yet"), which leads to adding fields, objects and mechanisms until the difficulty is closed. The other two readings have to be asked for on purpose, and the history shows that they are not even available unless asked (below).

M1 holds the two readings of [J7 The two readings of pressure](judgment.md#j7-the-two-readings-of-pressure) and adds the third; J7 sends a difficulty here when it is the formulation's. A design reading found here goes to the move that acts on it, not back to J7, which would ask this question again.

### Two levels: the question's premise and the problem's premise

"Formulation" covers two levels, and a difficulty can sit at either.

The first is the premise of a question. A question raised during construction is phrased in the vocabulary of the structure that raised it (which field, which carrier, which option), and it inherits that structure's premise. It cannot ask whether the structure should exist. Example from the source history (the reading enduring; the counts objective-bound, one reader's coding; [cases.md](cases.md), P17): of twenty-five questions raised while building one system, fifteen rested on a premise of their own representation and went away or changed when the represented object did; four were missing capabilities, two design faults, four the owner's choices. For thirteen of the seventeen questions marked as disappearing, the answer finally adopted was already among the question's own options; what was missing was not the option but the reason to take it. A reader of one such question put the opposite case exactly: "The three options listed in the question are all ways of re-attaching it" (agents' text of the source history; `cases.md` P17).

The second is the premise of the problem: what the system is answerable for. Example (the reading enduring, its figures objective-bound; P17): a requirement that the system's own queues and bookkeeping stay bounded entered a design, met construction at once, was kept by a review that improved how it was measured, and was built across most of a construction round, with typed size accounting on every model type. It was dissolved only when the owner asked of each commitment, "**Should the constraint it attempts to satisfy be a design requirement for [the system] at all?**" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-08; `cases.md` P17). Six days and sixty-one construction cycles passed between the requirement's first appearance in the code and that question, and it was found not from a failure but from reading where the effort of the cycles had gone.

The two levels need different questions. A question's premise is found by asking what object the question presupposes and whether the difficulty survives without it. A problem's premise is found by asking who relies on the property, in their words, and whether anything they rely on would be lost.

### Pressure at three levels, and the fates as the record

Example (enduring; P17): in one history, construction pressure was read at three levels in turn, and each was unavailable from the one below. The first triage had three outcomes (add a capability, settle locally, ask the owner) and no slot for "the representation is the cause". The next round added that reading, "the possibility that the specification is over-abstracted, rather than defaulting to the assumption that the implementation is incomplete" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-06; `cases.md` P17). The round after added the third: "previous refactoring efforts focused primarily on **how to implement established semantics more concisely**; the necessity of the semantic commitments themselves has not undergone the same level of scrutiny" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-08; `cases.md` P17). Adding a capability, removing a representation and relinquishing a requirement are three different acts. The middle one does not reach the third: a test that removes objects asks which boundary each object answers, and every bounded table answered one, so the bounds passed it. The object test and the requirement test are different tests, and a formulation trimmed by the first can still carry what only the second removes.

The fates are the readings written down. When inherited problems are re-asked of a concrete object ([formulation.md](formulation.md), F5), each receives a fate, and the fates record where the difficulty lay: in the formulation's ontology (not arising), with an existing supplier or another owner (dissolved, outside), or in the object being built (expands; carried, sometimes with a precision added). Example (objective-bound counts; P11): of 416 problems re-asked of a candidate system, 228 were carried, 106 expanded the design, 29 did not arise, 22 were dissolved by existing software, 19 were outside, 12 were the owner's. Three findings are easily merged and should not be. "The world answers it" (dissolved) creates a reliance, stated in one sentence the design must say. "Another owner holds it" (outside, or the owner's) names which owner. "The formulation produced it" (not arising) creates a boundary of applicability, the condition under which the problem would arise. They are different findings with different records.

**What it is not.** Not a partition of causes; a difficulty is a relation, and the reading chosen is a choice of where to act. Not a licence to reformulate first: the world question comes before the third reading, and a capability gap the world fills is a capability gap.

**When to reach for it.** When a question arrives in the form "which field, object or message should carry X". When a mechanism is expensive and the discussion is about making it cheaper. When the same difficulty returns after being fixed. When construction effort concentrates on one mechanism round after round.

**Signs you are violating it.** Repairs are made before anyone has said which reading they rest on. Every difficulty is filed as unfinished implementation. Questions are answered by choosing among their listed options. A capability is added that a stronger version of would still not make the difficulty go away.

**Worked application: a usage-billing service.** A cloud provider bills customers monthly from usage events. Events from some sources arrive days late (devices that were offline, batch uploads from partners). The team keeps meeting one difficulty: an invoice has been issued, and then usage for its month arrives. Construction has produced a "reopened" state for invoices, a regeneration job, credit-note logic for the difference, and a three-day hold before issuing that delays every invoice. The engineers' latest question is whether a regenerated invoice should be in the state draft, amended or superseded.

Asked of the world first, the difficulty as posed (how to reopen an issued invoice) has no ready answer; the answer the world holds returns below, once the problem is restated. Then read it three ways before answering. As capability: would faster ingestion remove it? It would shorten the late tail, but a device offline for a week reports a week late however fast the pipeline is; a stronger capability would not change the judgment, so this is not a capability gap. As design: is the system demanding an entity that need not exist? The reopened invoice is such an entity, and the question's three options are all ways of keeping it. That is the formulation at the question's level: the question asks how to represent a reopened invoice and cannot ask whether invoices reopen. As formulation at the problem's level: what is the system answerable for, and to whom? Customers rely on an issued invoice never changing, on each event being billed once, and on seeing when usage occurred. Finance relies on revenue being recognized in the period it was earned. Nobody relies on every event appearing on the invoice for the month in which it occurred; that was a convenient representation (invoice month equals usage month) promoted to a promise. Ask the world: utilities and telecom operators have long billed late usage on the next invoice with the usage date shown, and accrual accounting already recognizes revenue in the period earned through the ledger, independently of invoices. Finance's need is dissolved by an existing practice, with the reliance stated in one sentence ("late usage is accrued to its usage month by the ledger, not by the invoice").

Reformulate: an event is billed on the first invoice issued after it is known, labelled with its usage date. The reopened state, the regeneration job, the difference logic and the hold fall away together, and the engineers' question has no object left. What weakened, named: a customer may see last month's late usage on this month's invoice, which is now a stated property. And the condition under which the old problem would return is recorded: a contract that promises totals per usage month (some enterprise contracts do) brings the problem back for that contract, where it is that contract's term, not the system's default.

## M2 The operation

**Question.** Which reformulation does the evidence call for, and of which form?

**Consequence.** Name the operation and its form, because the forms have different tests. A re-rendering asks the same thing in a form that changes the cost of solving, and is tested by equivalence of what is asked. A replacement solves a different problem in the original's service, and is tested by whether its answer transfers back. A change of what is asked moves the ends, the claim or the level, and has no formal test: it is tested by whether those who rely on the answer accept it, and is recorded as such. Name the form for each party that relies on the answer: one change can be a re-rendering for one party and a change of what is asked for another, and the form says whose acceptance is owed. Choose the level of the reformulation with its cost on each side stated, and in a live system the cost of the transition as well (M1; [C4 Expand the platform, not the special case](principles.md#c4-expand-the-platform-not-the-special-case)). One reformulation at the right level is worth many repairs below it.

**Why.** "Reformulation" covers operations that preserve the set of answers, narrow it, or replace the problem, and the literature mostly treats them as one. Amarel's rewrites keep the problem and change its cost; Pólya's auxiliary problem is a different problem used for its result or its method; Rittel and Webber's choice of level, Schön's problem setting and Lakatos's changes of concept or domain change what is asked. Mixing them hides what must be checked. A re-rendering presented as a change of what is asked escapes the equivalence check it could have passed; a change of what is asked presented as a re-rendering claims an equivalence that does not hold, and the people who relied on the old answer find out later. Naming the form is what makes the record (M4) honest.

The operations below are those the source history used, grouped by form, with the case each came from (examples; see [cases.md](cases.md)) and the test that applies. A few come from the literature and are marked so.

### The level, and what operations cost

Rittel and Webber: "There is nothing like a natural level of a wicked problem ... the higher the level of a problem's formulation, the broader and more general it becomes: and the more difficult it becomes to do something about it. On the other hand, one should not try to cure symptoms". Too low, and a fix can make the higher problem worse; too high, and nothing can be done. The level is chosen, with its cost on each side stated. The common error at this choice is the failure pattern regrowth and the better mechanism, which [D4 Revise at the level of premises](principles.md#d4-revise-at-the-level-of-premises) holds. Example (enduring; P17): the bookkeeping bounds of M1 were first criticized for how they were measured, and the answer was better measurement; the owner later named the earlier rounds as having "merely introduce[d] better mechanisms for managing complexity that could have been avoided altogether" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-08; `cases.md` P17).

Operations cost. Moving a question to another owner needs a receiving owner that has the question in a usable form; in the record, a destination is often only a label ("to the knowledge layer", "to progression") that nothing checks. A matter moved to a producer's precondition is paid for in unchecked exposure: the property has become a parameter someone must supply. Where meaning is held by several parties or outside the organization (a statute, a contract, a field's conventions), a question moved to "the owner" names which holder, and the formulation says how each holder's changes reach the system (a regulator's waiver, a court's reading of a clause); the source history had one owner of meaning, and this is drawn from cases applied after it ([cases.md](cases.md), the trial). A replacement for a removed representation is a new representation and needs the same test: in the history, ten dissolved questions were replaced by nine named constructs, and four of those were withdrawn within days.

**What it is not.** Not a rename: words that move while behaviour and ownership stay are the failure pattern rename as revision. Not a replacement ontology: the owner's instruction when objects were removed was "do not simply create a 'better ontology' to replace it" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-07; `cases.md` P16).

**When to reach for it.** When M1 has given the formulation reading. When a reformulation is being proposed and nobody has said what form it takes or how it would be tested.

**Signs you are violating it.** A reformulation is claimed to be equivalent and nobody checked. A change of what is asked is made without asking those who rely on the answer. A question is "moved" to an owner that does not know it has it. The removed structure returns under a new name.

**Worked application: a delivery-route planner.** A courier plans daily van routes: parcels with addresses and time windows, vans with capacities, a cost per kilometre and per hour. The current formulation is one integer program for the whole city, re-optimized every five minutes as parcels arrive and guaranteed to return the cheapest plan, with two regional dispatch offices negotiating parcel swaps between their vans. It is slow, routes change under drivers, and the swap negotiation sometimes deadlocks.

The team's first reading was capability: "the re-optimizer is too slow", which invites a faster solver. Read at the level of what is asked, the question was whether loaded routes should be re-optimized at all. Ask who relies on what. Drivers rely on a route that does not change after the van is loaded; customers on their window; dispatch on knowing by mid-morning whether any window will be missed. Nobody relies on a cost minimum recomputed every five minutes. Restate: a route is stable once its van is loaded; new parcels go to vans not yet loaded or to an afternoon wave; the plan's cost is kept as an observation (reported, compared day to day) and dropped as a guarantee. Then remove a party: two offices negotiating swaps are a problem of agreement, and letting one planner decide swaps for both removes the negotiation, its deadlocks and its retries. These changes have no formal test, and their form differs by party: for customers, whose windows are kept, the restatement is a re-rendering; for drivers and dispatch it changes what is asked, and their acceptance is what is owed. The record says whether they gave it.

What remains is planning the vans not yet loaded, and the faster solver is not needed. A re-rendering makes that smaller problem cheaper: rewrite the time windows as a time-expanded network, in which waiting is an arc and windows bound the times at nodes; the feasible set is the same and the solver's relaxation is much tighter (Williams's point about sharp formulations). The test is equivalence: on past days' instances, the same plans are feasible and the optimal cost is unchanged. The drivers' separately granted freedom "to deviate when traffic requires", policed by an exception-approval flow, goes the same way by relocating a definition: a route is a set of stops with an order imposed only where windows require it, the rest of the order being the driver's. The test: every plan that met the windows still meets them.

No replacement was taken. Clustering parcels into zones and routing each alone was proposed early, and would be one, tested by whether zoned routes transfer back to the whole city; once loaded routes were stable, the planning left was small enough to solve whole, and the record names zoning as considered and not taken.

## M3 The signs

**Question.** Does the evidence show a formulation fault, and at which level?

**Consequence.** Each sign reopens the formulation at the level it names, not at the mechanism where it was noticed. A sign is where to look, not a proof.

**Why.** A formulation fault does not announce itself; it shows as difficulty that repair does not reduce, or that moves when repaired. The signs below are the forms in which such faults were recognized in the source history, grouped by the level they point to. This table is the list; [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) gives a few as examples. Their value is that they let a difficulty be read as evidence about the formulation before the next repair is made.

| Sign | What it usually points to | Reopen |
|---|---|---|
| A construction question whose premise rides in its options; a question phrased in the vocabulary of the structure that raised it | The question presupposes an object that may not need to exist | The question's premise |
| A question channel that keeps receiving questions of another level | The channel was formulated for one kind of question | The channel's criterion, not each question |
| A tension rediscovered several times under different vocabularies | A line drawn in the wrong place | Where the thing is defined |
| A problem answerable only by reintroducing an abstraction; a closed model that generates its own problems | A problem space treated as a parts list | The model's standing (map, not specification) |
| A status that answers an adjacent question; a ranking contradicted within a day | A representation of the problem taken for the problem | The representation |
| A word that names two problems | Two problems merged | The vocabulary, then each problem |
| A term with no provenance in earlier formulations; vocabulary carrying a neighbouring domain's meaning | An imported frame | The frame |
| A formulation whose closure needs conformance work on every substrate | Generality bought at the edges | What the formulation fixes |
| A requirement no party relies on; configuration offered for things that are not real resources | A convenient requirement | What the system is answerable for |
| A dissolved problem guarded by a prohibition where a test is wanted | A dissolution not yet trusted | How the dissolution is checked |
| A problem of agreement among parties where the object has one party | A party the formulation assumed | The parties |
| Effort concentrating on a mechanism that a stronger capability would not relieve | A requirement the mechanism serves | The requirement |
| A limit found at a join of a converged formulation: a closed enumeration at the join; a fixed procedure on one side that what the other side supplied had to fit; a missing meaning | A join with no semantics of its own | The join (L3) |

**What it is not.** Not proof. Not a list to sweep a design against; a sign is noticed in the course of work and read where it was noticed.

**When to reach for it.** When the same difficulty has been repaired more than once. When a discussion is about a mechanism's cost. When a question arrives with options.

**Signs you are violating it.** A sign is repaired at the mechanism where it appeared, and it returns elsewhere.

## M4 The record

**Question.** What premise changed; what disappears with it; what survives and where it now lives; what weakened and is named as weakened; what the original demanded and whether the new formulation still meets it; what of the old formulation is kept as material; and which form the reformulation takes for each party that relies on the answer?

**Consequence.** A reformulation without a record is a rename; a record without a premise is a patch. The record also names what the removed thing silently supplied, and treats any replacement for a removed representation as a new representation that needs the same test. In a live system the record also says which parts still run under the old formulation, a field a half-applied reformulation needs for as long as it lasts ([C4 Expand the platform, not the special case](principles.md#c4-expand-the-platform-not-the-special-case) for the migration). Example (contextual): in a practice organized in rounds, completed records are read-only and a later decision is recorded later.

**Why.** A reformulation can lose the original. Pólya warns that one may "stray so far from our original problem that we are in danger of losing it altogether", and gives the check, "Did you use all the data? Did you use the whole condition?"; Lakatos shows a concept redefined so a claim survives at the price of its meaning. Writing down what the original demanded, and checking the new formulation against it, is how the record guards against this. Naming what the removed thing silently supplied guards against the quieter loss. Example (enduring; P16): removing an account of why values were kept in memory exposed that nobody had ever said when to release them; the reduction in representation revealed an unspecified behaviour, and it had to be stated.

**At the scale of one problem.** When an inherited problem is re-asked of a concrete object, its record has six fields: the problem as posed; where it exists in the object; its fate; its answer; where the answer goes in the design; and what remains open. The problem is written in plain words stripped of the ontology that phrased it ("a situation the software must handle, a fact it must maintain, a boundary it must observe, a failure it must propagate, a choice it must leave to someone"), and the second field is filled before the third, so that a fate is assigned against the object rather than against concepts.

**At the scale of a formulation.** The record answers the question above, and names the form (M2), because the form says which test was owed. When the evidence came from a build, its verdict is triaged before it is adopted: "simplifications that can be formally adopted, omissions made solely due to implementation scope, and conclusions that remain unsupported" (the owner of the source history, September 2026; `cases.md` P5b). A narrowed build drops structure for reasons of scope, and only the first group is evidence about the formulation.

**What it is not.** Not a decision ledger kept beside the work as a second ontology of resolved answers; the record is short, it is written where the reformulation is decided, and the formulation itself carries the result. Not amendment of earlier records to fit the new formulation.

**When to reach for it.** Whenever a formulation changes, and whenever a problem receives a fate.

**Signs you are violating it.** A reformulation is announced and nobody can say what fell away. A removed structure returns because nobody wrote what it was for. An old record is edited to agree with the new formulation.

## L1 Which loop

**Question.** Am I forming the problem, building the system, or reading the system back into the problem; at which tempo; and what carries the result across?

**Consequence.** Name the loop and the tempo. Neither loop runs to completion before the other starts. A switch between them states its reason by naming what the preceding mode has been doing and saying that it is not the question. A change to a definition others rely on is decided by whoever holds that definition, with its record. Semantic consequence draws the line: a change of what is asked goes to the holder of the meaning; a re-rendering or a replacement, which leaves what anyone relies on unchanged, is engineering and is recorded under M4. The criterion is itself a formulation of the channel through which problems reach the holder (the failure pattern *the channel's premise*).

**Why.** Brooks, Rittel and Webber, and Simon agree that a formulation is revised throughout the work, not once; "one cannot first understand, then solve". A process organized as a pipeline with a fixed first stage either freezes a formulation that construction will show wrong or never builds. And the two loops carry different things: the problem hands construction a fixed meaning, guarantees and a concrete object (L2); construction hands back questions with their premises, counts of what disappeared and the effort mechanisms induce (L3). Naming the loop says which of these is being produced.

[D2 Construction is evidence](principles.md#d2-construction-is-evidence) and the account of working with others ([writing-and-working.md](writing-and-working.md#part-ii-organizing-the-work)) are the twins; which loop a unit of work is in is stated here, and they point to it. Example from the source history (objective-bound; `cases.md` P11, P17): one owner held the meaning and defined its principled world, and the language, the operating system, the existing stacks and the engineers' judgment completed the rest.

### Tempos

A formulation is revised at three tempos, and confusing them is costly. A solver revises its view of the world at every event: short-term and reversible, Simon's "adaptation". Knowledge (policies, cost models, candidate implementations) is revised between runs, outside the live solving where the meaning must hold for a run ([recursion.md](recursion.md), R5 and R6). The problem itself is revised between rounds of work, deliberately and with a record. A revision made at the wrong tempo is the error: a solver that changes what is asked while running has no fixed meaning to answer to (one that adds lemmas without changing what is asked has not changed its problem), and a problem revised on every event is not formulated at all.

### The alternation, and the form of a switch

Example (objective-bound as an organization, enduring as a statement; P17): in one history, ten switches between design, construction, reverse pressure and trimming each came with a reason in the owner's words, and none was triggered by a failed build, a failing test or an outside review; the triggers were patterns the owner read in the tree and its history. The interval shortened as the work matured (one day; then forty-six construction cycles; then fifteen). The switches from construction back to the design had one form: they named what the preceding mode had been doing and denied that it was the question. "The central question is not whether the current implementation is literally faithful to the specification, nor is it to continue filling in objects, fields, schemas, or protocols that remain incomplete in the specification" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-06; `cases.md` P17).

The order of settlement in the same history (contextual, and resting on the lead's account alone): the floor was settled under a narrow action space and a fixed procedure, and only then were the strategy and the action space widened "without reopening" it ([cases.md](cases.md), P18).

**What it is not.** Not a pipeline with a fixed first stage. Not alternation on a schedule.

**When to reach for it.** At the start of a session or round. When work has gone on in one mode for a long time and its outputs have changed character. When a question arrives and it is unclear whether it is the owner's.

**Signs you are violating it.** Construction continues while its questions accumulate unread. A design is perfected while nothing is built against it. The owner receives engineering questions, or engineers decide what the system is answerable for.

## L2 What the problem hands over

**Question.** Does construction receive a fixed meaning, a few guarantees and a concrete object explicit enough that its limits can be named, or a list of problems to realize item by item?

**Consequence.** A list of problems is a coverage map, never a parts list; what is built answers to the object and the flows through it. The unit of work is a change to the object stated in the object's terms ("which table/field/message/state/flow step changes, from what to what"), and a source of work that cannot be so stated "is not yet a change; it is recorded and not worked" (agents' text of the source history; `cases.md` P10). A limit is findable only against an explicit, converged formulation. What tests may define is [D3 No second specification](principles.md#d3-no-second-specification).

**Why.** A list hands construction the representation of the problem instead of the problem, and construction then builds the representation. A concrete object lets each problem be asked where it exists, and lets a flow traverse independently written parts until a step cannot be written ([formulation.md](formulation.md), F5 and F6; [D1 A concrete object early](principles.md#d1-a-concrete-object-early) and [D3 No second specification](principles.md#d3-no-second-specification)).

**What it is not.** Not a pilot or prototype handed over in place of a formulation. Not a parts list.

## L3 What construction hands back

**Question.** For each difficulty met in building, which premise did it assume, does the difficulty survive the removal of that premise, and what does the effort spent say about the requirement?

**Consequence.** Count what disappears with a representation; read what remains three ways (M1); carry it back with its record (M4). The carriers are several: a question with its premise examined; a flow traversing independently written parts until a step cannot be written; a survey of real uses against the formulation (what was used, what was spelled around it, what it cannot cover); a prototype, triaged; research against a converged formulation; the owner reading patterns in what has been built and in its history; and the effort a mechanism induces. The repair of a limit found at a join gives the join its own semantics, owned once, and enlarges neither side.

**Why.** Construction is the most literal reading a formulation gets. Its evidence comes in forms that are easy to discard: a question that looks like a request for a field, a count of lines deleted, the fact that most construction cycles mention one mechanism. Read as evidence, they say where the formulation is wrong. Example (enduring; P18): five research packages, each written against a converged design, found eleven limits within a day, with no failure and no execution; ten were at joins between parts and one inside a part, and the limits at joins took three shapes (a closed enumeration at the join; a fixed procedure on one side that content on the other side had to fit; a missing meaning). The repair was to give the joins their own semantics, without giving them runtimes: "[the five parts of the system] do not necessarily correspond to five independent software entities or runtimes" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-09; `cases.md` P18).

**What it is not.** Not a review loop and not a verification campaign; the carriers are read for what they say about the formulation, not counted as progress.

## L4 The floor

**Question.** What has survived every reformulation so far, and does this one touch it?

**Consequence.** The floor is what later reformulations stand on. It is written as the exceptions to the current reformulation, each with the failure it guards. It survives by being restated, not by reference to where it was first decided. A reformulation that touches it is a new system, and says so. The floor that [B4 Facts by construction, checks at boundaries](principles.md#b4-facts-by-construction-checks-at-boundaries) and the joins with real resources set ([judgment.md](judgment.md#the-counter-pass-and-the-floor)) is the same floor seen from the system.

**Why.** Reformulation that removes commitments has a direction, and it overshoots. The owner's guard, given at the same moment as the instruction to trim: "avoid trimming for the sake of trimming" (the owner of the source history, 2026-10-08; `cases.md` P17). A floor stated as a principle ("keep what is essential") does not stop the overshoot, because every removal looks inessential to the person making it. A floor stated as specific exceptions, each with the failure it prevents, can be checked removal by removal.

### The floor written as exceptions

Example (objective-bound content, enduring structure; P16, P17): in one history, what survived every reformulation was, as one list: the fixed meaning; the path for loss; the trust boundary; the lifecycles of what the world keeps alive. That content follows from that system's objectives, and another system's floor will differ. Its form is what transfers: each reformulation there that removed commitments carried a list of what it would not remove. One listed eight gaps a removal could not reopen (a controller identity that survives reconnection; a fence against a stale decider; evidence that a fact was applied, not merely received; and others), with the rule that a removal reopening one "is refused". The next listed nineteen items that "must stay" in strict form, among them no reuse of memory or devices while native access may continue, one holder per run, keys created once, and "Silence is never an ending" (agents' text of the source history; `cases.md` P17). The form is the point: each item names a failure (a silently wrong result, corrupted native state, a resource reused while still in use) rather than a mechanism, so a later reformulation can replace the mechanism and still be checked against the item.

### The counter-pass

The counter-pass of [judgment.md](judgment.md#the-counter-pass-and-the-floor), made on a design, applies to a reformulation with its object changed: the reader who did not make the reformulation reads the result against what the original demanded, the reliance each dissolved problem created and the floor's exceptions, and tries to force things back. Example (enduring; P16 to P18): the shape recurred three times in one history: a trimming draft over-reached at the floor, and a reading by someone else restored what it had removed at the joins with real resources. The check is Pólya's at the scale of a system: did the new formulation use all the data, and the whole condition?

**What it is not.** Not a list of what was decided before, and not a reason to keep a mechanism; the floor holds failures to be prevented, and any mechanism that prevents them will do.

**When to reach for it.** Whenever a reformulation removes something. When a trimming pass has momentum.

**Signs you are violating it.** A removal is justified by a principle and not checked against named failures. The floor is cited by reference to an old document rather than restated. Nobody but the author has read the reformulated result.
