# Problem and system design

## The stance

Design is deciding what must exist. A structure belongs in a system when something real requires it: a responsibility that someone relies on, a boundary where ownership, execution, trust, time or knowledge actually changes, a failure that someone would see, a party that acts on a distinction. Structures that exist because they make the system easier to describe, to verify or to reason about are the designer's scaffolding; they belong in the design's language, not in the running system. This is not a preference for less. The target is a more capable system with less management: a small explicit core whose foundation widens as needs arrive, the existing world (operating system, language, libraries, services, hardware) carrying what it already carries, and no parallel representations, re-verification, imposed lifecycles or distinctions nobody acts on.

A problem for a computational system is a representation, and it can be wrong. This Skill is a way of holding a problem so that its representation can be wrong without the work being lost: fix the meaning of what is to be computed in a representation that exposes the structure a solver needs and leaves realization free; state what the system is answerable for in the words of those who rely on it; name the problem's parts in the vocabulary of fields that already exist and ask each part of the world; ask the problem of a concrete object instead of closing it with concepts; where its sub-problems are of one kind, expand it by a derivation and not by a structure; read every difficulty three ways before repairing it; and reformulate as an act with a record. Forming the problem is not a stage that runs before design, not a catalogue of problems to cover and not a prescription of recursion.

Problem formulation and system design are two loops that change each other, and neither precedes the other or completes before the other starts. The problem guides construction as a fixed meaning, a few guarantees and a concrete object, and construction returns evidence about the problem through questions whose premise is the defect, through what has to be added to keep a concept alive, and through the count of what disappears when a representation does. The methodology is a set of principles and a set of moves, each move a question with a consequence. It is not a checklist: reach for the question the situation raises, and let its consequence change the design.

An agent designing a system is especially prone to four things this Skill exists to catch: closing each problem by adding an abstraction, so that concepts end up explaining concepts; building checks, ledgers and registries that become the goal; filling a schema completely because completeness feels like closure; and naming a thing into existence because it was useful to think with. Each looks like rigour. Each costs the system capability, because what it adds must then be maintained, kept consistent and defended. An agent formulating a problem is especially prone to four others: stating the problem in the shape of the mechanism it already expects; answering a question as posed when its premise is the defect; treating a list of problems as a list of parts to build; and turning the words it used to understand a problem into things the system must contain.

## What the result looks like

### A well-formulated problem

The moves below tend to produce a recognizable kind of formulation. It is the target:

- **A fixed meaning** in a small representation, stated once, with what it leaves free said as plainly as what it fixes. An answer that is an estimate (a score, a label near a boundary, a verdict that varies between runs) is a fact with a source and an uncertainty that choices read, never a term of the fixed meaning.
- **A short statement of answerability**: what the system is answerable for, to whom, in their words, with the measure of the whole beside it and binding nothing.
- **Parts named in the world**: each part named in the vocabulary of an existing field and asked of what already exists; the system's own problem is what remains between the parts.
- **Sub-problems, where the problem has them of one kind**: of the same kind as the whole, named by description, derived when needed, shared by identity, answered by one procedure, and stopping at atomic cases chosen on purpose. Where the parts are coupled, the coupling is stated; where the problem is not self-similar, it is not expanded recursively at all.
- **A concrete object** the problem is asked of, and flows through it that force its parts to touch.
- **A map of the problem space** kept beside the work as a lens and a coverage instrument, with its premises and blind spots written on it.
- **A statement of decided absences**: which of the formulation's absences are decisions, and what each would cost.
- **A record of every reformulation**: the premise changed, what fell away, what survives and where, what weakened, and which form the reformulation took for each party that relies on the answer; and a floor written as the exceptions to the current reformulation.

### A system designed this way

The principles below tend to produce a recognizable kind of system. It is the target, described from its abstractions to the product:

- **Abstractions.** A short statement of what the system is answerable for and a short list of guarantees in its users' words; a small, explicit semantic core, open at its boundaries, with latitude written into its definitions so that the choice space is as wide as meaning allows.
- **Module boundaries.** Boundaries where ownership, execution or trust change, not where a concept can be named; flat type spaces over recursive ownership; relations kept as relations.
- **Execution structures.** A few kinds of thing that exist at runtime, each at a real boundary with one owner; one recovery story; lifecycles left to their mechanisms; safety rules few and strict, and everything else ordinary engineering.
- **Organization.** Layers closed at their scale, each adding to the one above; views kept distinct; the source tree a maintenance structure, not a projection of the concept model; a design that explains itself constructively, states each thing once, marks the kind of each statement and keeps its history beside it.
- **The product.** Capabilities that grow by widening the foundation, built in large coherent steps toward the final form, with tests that confirm it and do not define it.

## The working loop

These are the steps of forming a problem and designing the system that solves it, in the order they usually matter. Return to an earlier step whenever a later one shows a premise was wrong; most of the value of the loop is in the returns. For an existing system the loop usually enters at step 7, with what has been built as evidence, and returns to steps 2 to 4 from there.

1. **Start from the need, in its own words.** Write what someone needs computed, decided or kept true, before any mechanism appears. If the need arrives already shaped as a mechanism ("build a scheduler", "add a recursive planner"), separate the restriction it implies from the mechanism it expects, and keep the expectation as a hypothesis. A problem formulated so that one mechanism answers it everywhere is already half a design, and the half was not examined.

2. **Fix the meaning, name the responsibilities and derive the guarantees (F1, F2, A1, A3).** Choose the representation: what it fixes, what it leaves free, what each restriction lets a solver derive. Write the few things the system is answerable for, in the words of the people and systems that rely on it, and the measure of the whole. The count is small. The system's purpose (for one system, efficient use of hardware) is the measure by which the whole is judged, not a guarantee, and it binds no construction below it. An aim binds nothing while it is an aim; stated as something a named party observes and relies on, with its scope and bound (typing does not wait on extensions; one team's volume does not degrade another's; cost grows in a way a team can see and steer), it is a guarantee and is written as one. Cost, responsiveness and data protection enter the design this way, as responsibilities with a party. These two statements, the fixed meaning and the answerability, are what the rest of the work stands on, and the design holds them as A3 and A1: each later structure has to trace back to one of the responsibilities, and a structure that cannot is a candidate for removal. From each responsibility, state what a user or operator can rely on, once, where they would look for it. Name any guarantee you are deliberately weakening and what a user would see. Everything else in the design is a chosen means that may change while the guarantees hold, which is what leaves the implementation room to improve.

3. **Name the parts in the world, and ask the world first (F3, F4, B1, J6).** Test each element of the formulation for whether it belongs to the problem or was attached by a solution, a neighbouring domain, an earlier design or a convenient representation. Name the parts in the vocabulary of existing fields and ask the world first: does something that exists already solve each? For each problem, ask what already exists that carries it: the operating system's processes and files, the language's ownership and types, the database's transactions, the queue's redelivery, the platform's lifecycle. Many problems dissolve. What dissolves leaves; what remains between the parts is the system's own problem, and it is designed as an ordinary software structure before any new general concept is considered. The system owns the knowledge only it has.

4. **Form a concrete object early, and ask the problem of it (F5, F6, F7, D1, J18).** Draft a candidate made of things a reader can argue with: processes, tables with columns, messages with fields, states with transitions, steps with an actor. Make it the coordinate system for each later question. Hold it as a reference, not a verdict: nothing in it is sacred because it exists. Without an object, questions can only be answered by more concepts, and the design grows abstractions that explain one another. Ask each inherited problem in what concrete form it exists there, and give it a fate. Keep the problem list beside the object as a map, never as the parts list of the object. Say which absences are decisions.

5. **Expand the problem where expansion is its form (R1 to R7).** Where sub-problems are of the same kind as the whole and nearly independent, expand by derivation from the fixed meaning and the current facts, name sub-problems by description so that sharing follows, and stop at atomic cases chosen on purpose. Where the sub-problems opened are chosen by the value of what they would reveal (search, sampling, triage), the expansion and its stopping are policies, and only what each sub-problem is follows from the fixed meaning. Where sub-problems are coupled, state the coupling. Where a step is a one-shot external effect, it is not expanded. Where only one party decides, the recursion of agreement among parties does not start. A fact only a remote party knows is observed, not derived. Recursion is a property of how a problem is expanded against a fixed meaning; it earns nothing in the runtime by itself.

6. **Organize by closure (C1, C2, C4, J14).** Describe the system as closed systems at a few scales and from a few views, not as one tree. Each layer should be complete in its own terms, so that a reader who stops there holds a correct system at that scale. Lower layers add and realize; they do not retell. Put boundaries where ownership, execution or trust actually change. Before adding a mechanism for a need, ask what family of needs it belongs to and what foundation (a contract each member meets, a construct of the language, a premise) would make the family expressible.

7. **Construct, and read construction and every difficulty as evidence, three ways (D2, J7, J13, M1, M3).** Trace real paths through the design in time order and build it in large coherent steps toward its final form. When building strains (fields added to satisfy an abstract object, mappings kept to preserve an identity, a second lifecycle beside a native one), and whenever a difficulty appears, read it three ways before repairing anything: the system may lack a necessary semantic, the design may demand an entity that need not exist, or the problem as formulated may manufacture the difficulty. The world question of step 3 comes first; then ask whether the difficulty survives the removal of the premise it rests on. A probe that answers a question about the world (how a library behaves, what volume the first users bring) is research, not a pilot, and its shape is not kept as the architecture. In a system with live users, each step leaves the system serving.

8. **Revise at the level of premises, and reformulate with a record (D4, J9, J12, M2, M4).** When the design is heavy, ask first whether the constraint a mechanism serves should be a requirement at all. One changed premise that removes ten mechanisms is worth more than ten trims. A premise that should not be a requirement is a reformulation of the problem: name the operation and its form for each party that relies on the answer, and write what changed, what disappears, what survives and where, what weakened, and what the original demanded. Each removal names the behaviour that must still hold and what carries it now, and nothing removed comes back under another name. Safety claims and the joins with real resources are the floor below which trimming does not go.

9. **Switch loops with a reason (L1 to L4).** When the work moves between forming the problem, building the system and reading the system back into the problem, say which loop it is entering and why.

## The understandings of the problem side

The words problem, sub-problem, solver and recursion in this Skill are words for understanding a problem. None of them says that a system should contain an object, a hierarchy or a protocol by that name. Where a running system does carry something recursive, a real boundary is what warrants it: [B2 A concept is not an object](references/principles.md#b2-a-concept-is-not-an-object) lists them (another process or machine, time or restart, concurrent owners, an asynchronous completion, untrusted input, an external lifetime, state only a remote party knows, a public contract), and [R3 Derived, not stored](references/recursion.md#r3-derived-not-stored) gives the literature's reasons for an entity per unit, among them concurrency in the world and a failure to contain.

**What a computational problem is.** A problem is solvable by a computational system when its meaning is fixed in a representation that exposes the structure a solver needs and leaves realization free; when what the system is answerable for has been stated in the words of those who rely on it; when its parts can be named in the vocabulary of fields that already exist; and when it has been asked of a concrete object instead of closed by concepts. The chief act of formulation is a restriction: it gives up expressive freedom to gain structure for the solver and freedom for the realization. Well posed is a relation between a problem and a solver, not a property of the problem alone. Everything a formulation carries beyond its restriction is either intrinsic to the problem or attached by some solution, and the difference can be tested.

**Recursive expansion and the reuse of mechanisms.** A problem is recursively expanded when its sub-problems are of the same kind as it and one procedure answers every level. Where the facts it needs can be read back, the expansion is a derivation from the fixed meaning and the current facts, recomputed when needed and not stored; sub-problems have identity by description, so equal descriptions are one sub-problem and sharing follows from identity; where sub-problems are shared, the structure is a graph, not a tree; the recursion stops at atomic cases chosen on purpose, and moving an atomic boundary is a change of representation, made outside the live solving where the solver's control or the fixed meaning must hold for a run. The recursion of the problem is not the recursion of the solver's resources, code, processes or native systems, and several things may recurse side by side with no correspondence among their levels. The form applies where sub-problems are self-similar and nearly independent. Where they are coupled, the recursion organizes a coupled problem and does not pretend the parts are independent; where choice among a few whole alternatives matters more than division, the expansion earns little. Where a step is a one-shot external effect, it is not expanded; where only one party decides, the recursion of agreement among parties does not start; a fact only a remote party knows is observed, not derived.

**Reformulation and the evolution of understanding.** A formulation is revised when evidence shows that the difficulty at hand is the formulation's. Every difficulty is read three ways: as a capability the system lacks, as a design that demands something that need not exist, or as a formulation that manufactures the difficulty, at the level either of a question's premise or of the problem's. The three readings are three places to intervene, chosen by which change is cheapest and least damaging to what depends on the thing changed. Where the formulation manufactured the difficulty, intervening there is cheapest once the change is made, because the mechanisms that served it fall away together; in a live system whose parts move one at a time, the cost of the transition is stated separately, since it can exceed the repairs it replaces. Reformulation is not the correction of an error. The earlier formulation was the representation the work needed then, and what it produced is kept as material.

**The two loops.** The problem guides construction as a fixed meaning, a few guarantees and a concrete object explicit enough that its limits can be named, and not as a list of problems to realize item by item. Construction returns evidence about the problem: questions whose premise is the defect, fields and objects added to keep a concept alive, what disappears when a representation does, the effort a mechanism induces, and limits that research against a converged formulation finds at its joins. The work alternates between forming, building and reading back, and each switch states its reason. Across reformulations a floor survives (objective-bound, in the history this Skill was drawn from: the fixed meaning; the path for loss; the trust boundary; the lifecycles of what the world keeps alive), and a reformulation that touches the floor is a new system and is said to be one.

## The principles

Nineteen principles in four groups. Each is stated with what it is not in [references/principles.md](references/principles.md), because a principle without its over-correction is a slogan, and designers over-correct.

### A. What the system is answerable for

- **A1 Responsibilities before mechanisms.** Begin from the few things the system is answerable for and the guarantees they imply; everything else is a replaceable means. The purpose binds no construction; it is the measure by which the whole is judged, and an aim becomes a guarantee only where a named party relies on it. *Why:* without this, mechanisms justify one another and nothing tells you which can go.
- **A2 Effectiveness over completeness.** The objective is the whole system's effect in the world, not a complete description, uniform mechanisms or a better local metric. *Why:* completeness and uniformity are cheap to measure and easy to optimize, so they displace the goal.
- **A3 Fix meaning, free execution.** Specify what is computed or promised with only the constraints identity and correctness need; leave how to whoever can choose with the most information. *Why:* freedom written into the definition needs no policing; freedom granted separately grows an edge and a hidden second decider.

### B. What exists already, and what the system must add

- **B1 Design inside the existing world.** Ask the operating system, language, libraries, services and hardware first; design what remains as real structure; own only the knowledge only you have. *Why:* rebuilding what exists creates a second, weaker copy of semantics someone else maintains.
- **B2 A concept is not an object.** A named concept gets runtime identity, schema, lifecycle, registry or protocol only where it crosses a real boundary, a public contract its consumers depend on included. Problem, decomposition, solver and recursive process are design-time words: where the facts it needs can be read back, a problem's recursion can be derived rather than stored (R3 Derived, not stored), and what crosses a real boundary is stored; by itself it gets no tree, process or registry. *Why:* each reified concept must be created, stored, kept consistent and checked, and none of that serves a user.
- **B3 One fact, one owner.** Each fact has one authoritative carrier; other views are derived on demand. *Why:* each independent representation of one fact is a consistency obligation by construction.
- **B4 Facts by construction, checks at boundaries.** Make properties hold through the program's structure; check where something crosses a trust boundary or where a wrong claim would corrupt data or a resource. *Why:* a check against a fact the system itself produced can only catch the writer contradicting itself, and it is paid on each act.
- **B5 One path for loss.** One story for what happens when a part is lost, which all failures reach; no second defence against a failure this path covers and would report. *Why:* each extra recovery path is rarely exercised, interacts with the others, and is where the bugs live.
- **B6 Lifecycles belong to their mechanisms.** Do not couple things the world keeps alive separately, neither by promising survival nor by forcing destruction. *Why:* coupling either way builds machinery to stretch or cut a lifetime that already had an owner.

### C. How the whole is organized

- **C1 Closure at each scale.** Closed systems at several scales and views, each complete in its own concepts over the problems it owns; a problem is closed where it is answered, not where it is handed to a new abstraction, interface or external prerequisite; lower layers add, not retell; the layout is not the design. *Why:* a reader can then hold a correct system at any depth, and a change lands in one place.
- **C2 Boundaries from responsibility, not symmetry.** A process, service, protocol, lifecycle or top-level area exists where ownership (a team's included), execution or trust changes, not because a concept can be named or a taxonomy wants a row. *Why:* each boundary costs a protocol and a failure mode; symmetry pays that cost for nothing.
- **C3 Distinctions as fine as their consumers.** A state, outcome, reason or kind is a separate case only where some party acts differently on it. *Why:* each extra case must be produced, carried, tested and kept stable, and finer detail serves people better as text.
- **C4 Expand the platform, not the special case.** Meet a need by widening the foundation so capabilities grow with it; build in large coherent steps toward the final form. An absent capability is not a prohibition, and a possible need is not a reason to embed complexity now. A probe that answers a question about the world is research, not a pilot. In a system with live users, each step leaves the system serving, and moving units from old to new while the old drains is migration, not a compatibility layer, provided no unit is governed by both. Moving the atomic case of the problem (R5 The atomic case) is one way the foundation widens, done outside the live solving where the meaning must hold for a run. *Why:* special mechanisms accumulate into a system nobody can extend, and pilots set architecture by accident.

### D. How the work is done, and how it revises

- **D1 A concrete object early.** Form a candidate that can be argued with, and ask each question of it. *Why:* procedures that search for structure without an object generate concepts that explain one another.
- **D2 Construction is evidence.** Read building difficulty as evidence about the design, with two possible conclusions. *Why:* construction is the most literal reading a design gets, and it shows where the design asks for things that need not exist.
- **D3 No second specification.** Tests, checks, registers, plans, coverage maps and layouts serve the work and do not define it; the design and the product are the only sources of what the system is. The work's own apparatus is held to the same rule: no management layer, one independent reading of each version, state recovered from the design and the product. *Why:* whatever can say what is correct beside them becomes the target people optimize for.
- **D4 Revise at the level of premises.** Change the premise before trimming mechanisms; name what carries each removed behaviour; guard the floor. *Why:* mechanism-level trims leave the premise in place, and the premise regrows what was trimmed.
- **D5 The design explains itself.** Constructive description, each thing once where owned, each sentence of a plain kind, the history beside the design and not inside it. *Why:* when the design is the authority for construction, an explanatory sentence becomes a condition, and the condition becomes a mechanism.
- **D6 Escalate by semantic consequence.** Take a question to whoever owns the system's meaning only when its answer would change a long-term definition; decide everything else as engineering. *Why:* escalating by difficulty stalls the work and hands meaning-holders questions whose premise may be the defect.

## The moves

Forty-two questions in eight groups, each with the consequence it carries: twenty-two on the problem side, in the groups F, R, M and L, and twenty judgment moves on the design side, J1 to J20. Depth, cases and worked applications are in the references named at the end; the judgment moves' depth, reasons and worked applications are in [references/judgment.md](references/judgment.md), along with the procedure for deciding whether to expand, reorganize, absorb or remove a mechanism.

### F · What a computational problem is

| Move | Question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 The fixed meaning | What does this formulation fix, in what representation, and what does it leave free? What does each restriction let the solver derive, and whose choice does each fixing remove? | Write the formulation as two lists, what it fixes and what it leaves free. A restriction earns its place by what the solver derives from it. Four parts: a criterion for an acceptable answer; a representation of the situation and the permitted moves; a practicable amount of computation; and a link to the world the answer serves, which no internal check will catch and which is checked by varying what the representation holds fixed and the world does not. Each matter has one home: fixed by the formulation; bound when the environment is known (the machine a run meets, the facts a case brings); or decided during operation; nothing moves earlier because it could. Nothing of a solution's facts flows up into the fixed meaning, nothing of the fixed meaning flows down to remove a solver's meaningful choice, and legality does not depend on what can execute the formulation today. Not minimalism: a construct enters when the solver must see it to choose well. |
| F2 Answerability | What is the system answerable for, to whom, in their words? What is the measure of the whole? Which statements are promises to a party and which are means of keeping them? | A few guarantees; an aim that a named party relies on, with its scope and bound, is one of them (A1 Responsibilities before mechanisms). Otherwise the measure binds no construction. Where the system's choices are made by strategies meant to improve, the measure is best stated as properties of the space in which they choose (what can be expressed, what crossing costs, what can be known), not as a score; a system with no improving strategies keeps an ordinary measure of outcome. The quality of particular choices belongs to the policies at decision points, not to the system's answerability. Keep promises apart from means so the formulation can say which promises would change if the means changed. A count of the formulation's own nouns is progress on nothing. |
| F3 Intrinsic or attached | For each element: is it in the problem, or an assumption of a solution, a neighbouring domain, an earlier design or a convenient representation? | A formulated problem is a question, an option space, constraints and an owner. Two tests do most of the work: the second-implementation test (what would an independent implementation need to behave the same at the boundaries and after loss, and what is one way of thinking the problem through written as a requirement?) and the would-it-emerge test (built today from the real behaviours and the substrates available, would this structure appear?). Openness, grounding, no leakage, the owner and membership questions, provenance and the cut between requirement and machine catch narrower attachments; they are in the reference. Remove an attached element and name what it was attached to. |
| F4 Named in the world | Can the problem's parts be named separately in the vocabulary of existing fields, and does something that exists already solve one of them? | A problem is mature when its parts can be named. Ask each named part of the world first; it may dissolve, with the reliance stated. The system's own problem is what remains between the parts (B1 Design inside the existing world says the same of the system). Where the world is itself software, it is the stacks that exist and their documented semantics. Existing systems are references for parts, not the architecture; supply the structure as a premise and test surveys against it. |
| F5 The concrete form | In what concrete form does this problem exist in the object at hand? | A problem answered against concepts is not yet formulated. Write it in plain words stripped of the ontology that phrased it, find where it lives in the object, and if it lives nowhere, ask what in the old form produced it. Each inherited problem meets a fate: dissolved by the world (state the reliance in a sentence), carried by an existing element, expands the design, does not arise (state the condition under which it would), the owner's (naming which holder of the meaning, and how that holder's changes reach the system), or outside. A fate may go unused. Where these fates and J18 The fates diverge, the names here govern, since a fate is a finding about the problem. Not a coverage exercise, and not a pilot whose shape becomes the architecture; a probe that measures the world is part of forming the problem. |
| F6 The map | Which representation of the problem am I working inside, what are its premises, and what can it not show? | A list, register, model, ranking or status is a lens to query and a coverage instrument, never a parts list. Its leaves are meant to be reformulated; its premises are its blind spot, and tests run inside it cannot test it. Agreement of independent cuts says where a problem is, not that it is one; closure over a set certifies relations among answers, not the set's ontology. Write the assumptions no section checks, so that a later reader can tell a failed assumption from a failed detail. |
| F7 Decided absences | Which absences of this formulation are decisions, and which are not yet reached? | Sort them: a decided absence is stated once, with what it would cost; an unreached one is left unstated, so that it hardens neither into a prohibition nor into a mechanism carried for a possible need (C4 and D5). Which forms come first is progression, not architecture. Not deferral of building, and not minimal implementation as the objective. |

*Why these moves:* a formulation carries more than its problem (the mechanism someone expected, a neighbouring domain's vocabulary, a representation that was convenient, the order in which work happened), and each move separates one of these from what the problem is, so that what remains can be solved and what was attached can be dropped without loss.

### R · Recursive expansion and the reuse of mechanisms

Recursion is one form a problem's expansion can take, not a property every problem has. These moves apply where sub-problems are of the same kind as the whole and nearly independent; R7 says what the form can still do where they are coupled. Where a step is a one-shot external effect, it is not expanded; where only one party decides, the recursion of agreement among parties does not start; a fact only a remote party knows is observed, not derived.

| Move | Question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Same kind, same procedure | Is this sub-problem of the same kind as its parent, and does one procedure answer every level without a new case? | What is uniform across levels is the procedure and what admits a sub-problem; the content differs per kind, and the kinds are few. If a level needs its own case, mechanism or vocabulary, the decomposition is not recursive there: the kind is wrong or the level is an atomic case. An answer that is an estimate (a score, a label near a boundary, a verdict that varies between runs) is a fact with a source and an uncertainty that choices read, never a term of the fixed meaning, and a wait may name the evidence that would narrow it. Design decomposition and composition together. Keep apart a recursion in the problem's definition, one in a method of solving and one in the runtime. Do not subdivide what can be completed directly in order to keep a recursive form. |
| R2 Identity by description | Can any party derive the same name for this sub-problem from the same fixed meaning and facts, and does the name hold exactly what changes the obligation? | Then sharing is a consequence, not a mechanism: equal descriptions are one sub-problem, work in flight serves all its consumers, and the structure is a graph. The name excludes scheduling history and the remainder, which is a function of the identity and the facts; where the fixed meaning is versioned while sub-problems are open, the version in force is part of the name. Sharing needs a name derived from the problem alone, an answer that depends only on the name and its named dependencies, and a record of what it depended on; an input the name omits makes reuse silently wrong. An allocated name can be resolved only through whoever minted it; across parties it needs a registry, where a derived name needs none. |
| R3 Derived, not stored | Is the expanded structure a derivation that can be recomputed from what is durable and what can be observed, or an object hierarchy that must be kept consistent? | Derive it when needed; store only what crosses a real boundary (durable progress, work in flight, choices adopted and not yet carried out, facts only a remote party knows). One procedure re-run on each event, whose answer may be "wait", can replace a tree of suspended work. An entity per sub-problem needs a reason the recursion does not supply and is bounded by a grain cost. The form holds where the facts the derivation needs can be read back: dependencies discovered by solving are recorded and derived over, and a meaning versioned while work is open is carried in the names (R2). |
| R4 Which recursion | Which of the things that recurse here is this: the problem's expansion, the construction of a computation, the mechanical composition of an accepted act, the nesting of native systems, the ownership of resources, the unfolding of a description, the layout of a repository? | Name each by what it may decide and where it ends, not by its shape. No structural isomorphism: a level of the problem is not a node, process, rank, pool or authority, and the depth of one recursion is not the depth of another. A coupling between two is a lowering at a named point, never a correspondence of levels. Recursion, hierarchy and decomposition are independent properties. |
| R5 The atomic case | Where does the expansion stop, by whose decision, and what is on the other side? | The stopping case is chosen, not found: it sits where the fixed meaning stops defining structure or another party owns the next level. Moving it is a change of representation: a new candidate (a primitive, a larger implementation, a new formulation) admitted and chosen among by the same procedure. Where the solver's control or the fixed meaning must stay unchanged for a run, the move is made outside the live solving; otherwise moving it during the solve is a design choice, recorded, with the version of the meaning each answer used. Lifting a sub-structure into a primitive is a bet that it recurs and that its internals will not be needed. Opaque by decision is distinguished from not yet looked at. |
| R6 Fixed derivation, free choice (the control test) | Which parts of the expansion does the fixed meaning determine, and which are choices? | The derivation is the same everywhere; a choice is a policy at a decision point. Where choices are supplied by parties outside the solver, a proposal is checked against the facts current when it returns, and the structure of choices and their openness to those parties are sized separately. Where sub-problems are opened for what they would reveal, the expansion and its stopping are policies. A domain's own "decision" (an adjudication) is a derivation where the facts determine it and a choice only where they do not. The test: vary what is meant to be only control (order, direction, parallelism) and see whether the answer changes; if it does, that structure is in the problem or is wrongly called control. |
| R7 Coupled or independent | Are these sub-problems independent, nearly independent, or coupled through a shared resource, a global constraint or an objective that does not separate? | Independent: expand and share. Nearly independent: expand, and carry the coupling as a fact at the boundary. Coupled: the recursion organizes a coupled problem and the coupling is stated; parts that must run concurrently while exchanging are one joint execution, not independent problems; a global feasibility proof over the whole expansion is a manufactured requirement. Where choosing among a few whole alternatives matters more than dividing them, measure before expanding. |

*Why these moves:* the recursion of a problem is easily mistaken for a structure the running system must hold, and the power of recursion (one procedure for every level, sharing that follows from identity) comes from the derivation, not from any tree that represents it.

### M · Reformulation

| Move | Question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| M1 The three readings | Is this difficulty a missing capability, a wrong design, or a formulation that manufactures it; and at which level, a question's premise or the problem's? | Answer before repairing. The order of readings: the world first (step 3, F4), then the three places read together. Capability: widen the foundation. Design: the design move that acts on it (J2 for an entity that need not exist, J3 for a second owner of one fact, B5 for a second path for loss, J12 for a premise several mechanisms share). Formulation: reformulate (M2); the mechanisms that served the old formulation fall away together, which makes this cheapest once the change is made, and in a live system that moves part by part the transition is costed separately (C4). Three findings stay apart: the world answers it (state the reliance); another owner holds it (name which); the formulation produced it (state the condition under which it would arise). Pressure is read at three levels, each invisible from the one below: add a capability; remove a representation; relinquish a requirement. If a stronger capability would not change the judgment, the difficulty is not one of capability. |
| M2 The operation | Which reformulation does the evidence call for, and of which form? | Name the operation and its form (re-rendering, replacement, or a change of what is asked; see the operations by form below) for each party that relies on the answer, since one change can be a re-rendering for one party and a change of what is asked for another. Choose the level and state its cost on each side; in a live system, state the cost of the transition too (C4). One reformulation at the right level is worth many repairs below it. |
| M3 The signs | Does the evidence show a formulation fault? | Each sign reopens the formulation at the level it names, not the mechanism. Among them: a tension rediscovered under several vocabularies; a question whose premise rides in its options; one word naming two problems; a requirement no party relies on; effort concentrating on a mechanism a stronger capability would not relieve. The list, grouped by the level each sign points to, is in the reference. A sign is where to look, not proof. |
| M4 The record | What premise changed, what disappears with it, what survives and where it lives now, what weakened, what the original demanded and whether the new formulation still meets it, what of the old is kept as material, and which form the reformulation takes for each party that relies on the answer? | A reformulation without a record is a rename; a record without a premise is a patch. Name what the removed thing silently supplied. A replacement for a removed representation is a new representation and needs the same test. At the scale of one problem the record is: as posed; where it exists; fate; answer; goes to; open. In a live system the record says which parts still run under the old formulation (C4 for the migration). In a practice organized in rounds, completed records are read-only and a later decision is recorded later. |

*Why these moves:* the cheapest repair of a difficulty is often in the statement, where the mechanisms that served it fall away together, and a reformulation without a record cannot be told from a rename or checked against what the original problem demanded.

### L · The two loops

| Move | Question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Which loop | Am I forming the problem, building the system, or reading the system back into the problem; at which tempo (the solver revising its view at each event, knowledge revised between runs, the problem revised between rounds of work); and what carries the result across? | Name the loop and the tempo. Neither loop runs to completion before the other starts. A switch states its reason by naming what the preceding mode has been doing and saying why that is not the question now. A change to a definition others rely on is decided by whoever holds that definition, with its record; semantic consequence is the line: a change of what is asked goes to that holder, while a re-rendering or a replacement is engineering, recorded under M4. This move owns which loop a unit of work is in; "Which loop" under working with others points here. |
| L2 What the problem hands over | Does construction receive a fixed meaning, a few guarantees and a concrete object explicit enough that its limits can be named, or a list of problems to realize item by item? | A list is a coverage map; what is built answers to the object and the flows. The unit of work is a change to the object stated in the object's terms; a source that cannot be so stated is not yet work. What tests may define is D3 No second specification. |
| L3 What construction hands back | For each difficulty met in building, which premise did it assume, does it survive the removal of that premise, and what does the effort spent say about the requirement? | Count what disappears with a representation; read what remains three ways; carry it back with its record. The carriers: a question with its premise examined; a flow traced through independently written parts until a step cannot be written; a survey of real use against the formulation; a prototype triaged into what to adopt, what was omitted for scope and what is unsupported; research against a converged formulation; the pattern of where effort concentrates. Repair a limiting join by giving the join its own semantics, owned once, not by enlarging either side. |
| L4 The floor | What has survived every reformulation so far, and does this one touch it? | Write the floor as the exceptions to the current reformulation, each with the failure it guards. It survives by restatement, not by reference. A trimming draft tends to over-reach at the floor, and a counter-pass restores it; a reformulation that touches it is a new system and says so. |

*Why these moves:* the problem and the system shape each other, and work that finishes one before starting the other either builds a list or reasons about an object nobody has built.

## Reformulating: the operations by form

Three different things go by the name "reformulation", and they are tested differently. A record names the form a reformulation takes for each party that relies on the answer, because the form says what would show it to be wrong.

| Form | What it does | Operations | Its test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Re-rendering | Asks the same thing in a form that changes the cost of solving | Relocate a definition so a boundary dissolves (a freedom granted separately becomes part of what an operation means); separate what was fused (a model from the conditions on its valid instances; responsibility from capacity; computation from execution; the several roles one unit was carrying); dissolve a construct into a relation (objects that stood for time become offsets in an index); re-level a choice to the layer that meets it; remove residence (a persistent controller becomes a one-off activation over persistent state) | Equivalence of what is asked: the new form answers every question the old one was relied on to answer |
| Replacement | Solves a different problem in the original's service | Widen (a scheduler becomes an execution model); expand into sub-problems of known kinds; specialize and relax (the literature's, with no instance in the source history) | Whether the answer transfers back to the original |
| Change of what is asked | Moves the ends, the claim or the level | Restate what the system is answerable for; ask whether a constraint should be a requirement at all; move a question to another owner; remove a party (one decider dissolves a family of agreement problems); keep the observation and drop the guarantee; recover an earlier intent at a higher level; supply a premise; demote a specification to a map (what is asked of the representation changes, and what is asked of the problem does not) | Nothing formal: only whether those who rely on the answer accept it, recorded as such |

The level is chosen, and it has a cost on each side. A higher formulation is broader and harder to act on; one too low treats symptoms and can make the higher problem worse. Moving a question to another owner needs a receiving owner that holds the question in a form it can use; a destination that is only a label has moved nothing, and a matter moved onto a producer's precondition is paid for in exposure nobody checks. Where meaning is held by several parties or outside the organization (a statute, a contract, a field's conventions), the formulation names each holder and how its changes reach the system. Any reformulation can lose the original: name what the original demanded, and check the new formulation against it.

## Failure patterns to match against your own formulation or draft

Why these are tempting. A problem stated in a neighbouring domain's vocabulary already has known solutions, so it feels tractable. A complete list of problems feels like understanding, and closing each entry feels like progress. A recursive problem drawn as a tree invites a tree of things to build. A property that can be checked feels like a requirement because checking it is easy to show. A registry feels like the place extensibility lives. Where the set of extensions is fixed when the system is built, a build-time list or the module system is the place; where third parties add extensions at run time, the registry crosses a real boundary and holds only what must be known before their code loads. A check feels like rigour and is easy to show correct, but a check against a fact the system produced only finds the writer disagreeing with itself. A generic layer over two mechanisms makes a diagram tidy, but it must then be maintained and it hides the difference that mattered. A complete schema feels like closure, but each closed gap adds an object whose own edges must be closed. When you notice the pull, ask who would be worse off if the element were not in the problem, and what would happen to a user if the thing did not exist.

Read your formulation or your draft against these. Each names the move or principle that answers it. They fall into three groups: patterns of the problem, of the structure, and of the work; the numbers are for reading only, and the patterns are cited by name.

### Patterns of the problem

1. **The imported frame, or the imported problem.** A model from a neighbouring kind of system manufactures problems this system does not have: a difficulty brought in with a neighbouring domain's vocabulary (transactions and exactly-once delivery; hard real-time bounds; per-run quotas) and then solved. A mechanism the frame names (retry, deduplication, rollback) is a sign to read, not a verdict. In the source history, a database's transactions, rollback, exactly-once delivery, retry and deduplication were imported into a computation whose world only moves forward; in such a world a repeat of a pure action named by its description is ordinary, and a repeat that samples a non-deterministic outcome is a different problem. (F3; M1; J1; J4; see also the convenient requirement and defensive drift)
2. **The separately granted freedom.** A degree of freedom (latitude) modelled as a grant whose edge must be policed instead of as a property of the definition, which becomes the hardest problem of the space. Dissolved by folding the freedom into the definition of the thing it applies to. (F1; A3; M2 relocate the definition)
3. **The parts list.** A problem space, register or model treated as a specification whose every entry must be realized, so that each problem is closed by an abstraction. (F6; L2; see also concepts explaining concepts)
4. **Concepts explaining concepts.** Each problem is closed by adding an abstraction; answers become circular. (D1, J15)
5. **Procedure before object.** Coordinators, cycles, registers and change rules set up to find a structure that does not yet exist. (D1)
6. **The closure drive, or the closure that makes its own problems.** Each gap designed to completion; objects appear whose edges must also be closed. Met in a formulation, it is one complete in itself whose every gap, when closed, opens another, because closure was measured by completeness instead of by what is answered. Stop at J4, not at completeness. (F5; F6; M3; J4; see also concepts explaining concepts and conditional promises)
7. **The adjacent answer.** A claim of settlement, or a status of "settled", that answers a neighbouring question; a filled schema taken for understanding. (F6; J18)
8. **The convenient requirement, and defensive drift.** A property made a requirement because it could be checked, described or enumerated, not because any party relies on it. Boundedness, determinism and formal completeness pursued until requirements of hard real-time or safety-critical systems creep into a system that is neither. (F2; F3 the second-implementation test; M1; J1; J4; see also the closure drive)
9. **The frozen envelope.** Questions of scope ("is X in scope at all", "which form first") treated as architecture; an absence read as a prohibition, or a possible future need taken as a reason. (F7; C4)
10. **The recollection as definition.** An earlier formulation inherited by memory or by reference and treated as fixed. Old questions may remain; an old ontology has no right of inheritance. (F3 provenance; F5; reopening on provenance is the opposite danger)
11. **Reopening on provenance.** A decision reopened because it differs from an earlier version or lacks an approval, not for a substantive reason.
12. **The level mismatch.** A question asked at the level of meaning that is engineering, or an engineering answer given to a question about what the system is answerable for. (L1; M2 re-level; D6; J10)
13. **The channel's premise.** A channel for questions formulated for one level keeps receiving questions of another. The repair is a criterion on the channel, not an answer to each question. (L1)
14. **The question in the structure's vocabulary.** A construction question phrased in the terms of the structure that raised it (which field, which carrier, which option) cannot ask whether the structure should exist; the premise rides in the options, and often the dissolving option is already in the list with no reason given to take it. (M1; M3; L3; J7)
15. **The lost intent.** A capability narrowed step by step while what lies beneath it is settled, until the original intent is forgotten; recovered once the floor is settled. Thinly attested: one episode, in the account of the lead who recovered it. (F2; L1; M2 recover an earlier intent)

### Patterns of the structure

16. **The recursion ontology.** The problem's recursion mistaken for the runtime's: a tree of workers, a process per level, a registry of sub-problems, capacity delegated down a tree, a global feasibility proof over it. (R3; R4; B2)
17. **The fused kinds.** Two things that recurse (responsibility and capacity; decisions and ownership; computation and execution) treated as one hierarchy because both recurse. (M2 separate what was fused; R4; see also uniformity for its own sake)
18. **The reification chain.** Fact, descriptive object, identity and lifecycle, copies, validation, reconfirmation. (J11)
19. **Uniformity for its own sake.** A generic layer over two differing mechanisms; an act forced into a vocabulary it does not belong to; a top-level area per concept. (J17, C2)
20. **The second axis.** A new classification dimension the system must then maintain everywhere. (J14, C2)
21. **The manufactured limit.** A bound on something that is not a real resource creates the failure it then needs a protocol for. (J4)
22. **Conditional promises.** A model complete in itself whose guarantees all depend on support nobody has committed to. (D1, J6)
23. **Rename as revision.** Words move while behaviour and ownership stay. Inside a set of parts changed together, a compatibility layer keeps two models alive; at a boundary with parties who cannot change with you (a public API, a file format, an extension contract), compatibility is a guarantee and is paid for there. (J9)
24. **Regrowth, and the better mechanism.** A removed thing returns under another name: a removed tracker returning as a "monitor", a removed state as a "hint". A critique of how a mechanism works invites a better mechanism where the question wanted was whether. On the problem side, the same pattern, which D4 names: a requirement criticized for how it is met and answered with better means, before anyone asked whether a party relies on it. (J9; D4; M1; M2 the level; see also the manufactured limit)
25. **Over-trimming at the joins.** Removal passes the point where a real resource needs its safety claim. (J9, B4)

### Patterns of the work

26. **The check layer.** Verification apparatus becomes a parallel semantics and the thing people optimize. (D3)
27. **Management for the work.** Plans, backlogs, status systems, ledgers and review layers that come to define the work. (D3)
28. **Polishing while unstable.** Editorial passes and terminology sweeps while the design still moves.

Recognizers of excess, for a quick pass over a design you suspect is heavy:

- a bound, budget, reservation or refusal on something that is not a real resource;
- a promise that holds in all interleavings where "usually, with a clear failure otherwise" would serve;
- an exact-byte, exact-order or closed-enumeration requirement nothing outside depends on;
- a check against a fact the system itself produced;
- a taxonomy of outcomes or reasons finer than any consumer acts on;
- certification or evidence demanded for things that would simply fail loudly.

The opposite error is real and different in kind. Over-defence adds mechanisms; over-trimming leaves a real resource outside a rule. When you remove something, name the real resource the removed rule used to cover.

## Writing a design

The full treatment is in [references/writing-and-working.md](references/writing-and-working.md). The essentials:

- **Statement kinds.** Each sentence is one of: a guarantee (what a user or operator relies on; few), a safety rule (strict, marked), a boundary form (a message, a durable record, a document another party writes), a mechanism (a chosen means that may change), or an explanation (binds nothing). Make the kind plain. Only guarantees, safety rules and boundary forms bind construction. A universal ("every", "never", "always") belongs in a safety rule, or in a guarantee that a structure makes true by construction; avoid it where meeting it would need a mechanism, because there it grows one.
- **Each thing once, where it is owned.** Define a concept in the highest layer where it can be stated whole, and link to it from elsewhere. Two statements of one rule drift into two rules.
- **Freedom is the default.** What the design does not state as a guarantee or a boundary form is the implementation's choice. Tuning values have defaults. The design does not list what it does not provide; where an absence is a decision a user relies on, it says so once, and it keeps kinds of limit apart (not yet designed, absent from this build, accepted limitation).
- **The record beside, not inside.** Why a decision was taken, who took it and what it replaced live in a record kept beside the design, one row per decision. The design states what the system is; history is not the reason it holds. Completed records are read-only; a later decision is recorded later.
- **Constructive, not defensive.** Describe what the system is and how its parts compose. Defensive wording and prohibitions against past mistakes turn into checks.

## Working with others

The full treatment is in [references/writing-and-working.md](references/writing-and-working.md). What follows is the organization that worked for large designs read and built by many agents under one owner of meaning; that form is objective-bound, so take from it what your scale needs. The parts that hold at any scale are one spine of names, one independent reading, escalation by semantic consequence, and state recovered from the design and the product.

- **Readers by boundary, judges by question.** Cut reading along the system's real boundaries, not as an inventory of objects. Give cross-cutting questions to judges, each judging against a list of behaviours written without the design's own nouns, so the list can protect what matters without protecting the nouns.
- **One spine.** Before parallel writing, fix one working document with the names, positions and placements all writers use. Parallel writers without one produce several designs.
- **One cross-reading.** After a version is formed, one independent reading by someone who did not write it, cut along lines that cross the parts, looking for contradictions between parts, regrowth and over-trimming. Then one fix pass. Judging, cross-reading and fixing are different tasks; what is avoided is the review-fix-review loop, which becomes the work.
- **Rulings on removals.** A ruling on a removal names what carries the behaviour now and what is lost; the full form the record prescribed also names what stays and what goes.
- **Escalation by semantic consequence.** Questions go to whoever owns the system's meaning only when the answer would change a long-term definition: a responsibility boundary, where authority lives, failure semantics, a public contract, a new core abstraction. Before escalating, ask whether the thing the question assumes should exist.
- **No management layer.** Direction, briefs, the design, the product and a short decision record are the whole apparatus. State is recovered from the design and the product, not from a ledger. Whoever owns the meaning intervenes by naming the kind of structure that is growing, not by fixing each instance.
- **Which loop.** A round, session or brief names which of the two loops it is in and what it hands the other; the rule is L1 Which loop.

## How the two sides join

The problem side and the design side are one methodology seen from two sides. The problem side asks whether an element is in the problem; the design side asks whether the system must carry it. They are written so that neither restates the other: where a move on one side has a twin on the other, the twin is named and not repeated.

| Problem side | Design side | The join |
|---|---|---|
| F1 The fixed meaning | A3 Fix meaning, free execution; B4 | The problem side chooses the representation and says what it fixes; the design side holds the line and frees execution. A check of the link to the world is not a check of a fact the system produced (B4). |
| F2 Answerability | A1 Responsibilities before mechanisms | One statement, written once: the problem side forms it, the design side derives guarantees from it. F2 carries A1's clause: an aim a named party relies on, with its scope and bound, is a guarantee. |
| F3, F4, F5 | J1, J6, J18, D1, B1 | The problem side asks whether an element is in the problem; the design side asks whether the system must carry it. The fates are shared vocabulary; where the two lists diverge, the problem side governs the names. |
| F6 The map | D3 No second specification | A problem representation is never a parts list; a design apparatus is never a second specification. |
| F7 Decided absences | C4, D5 | The design side holds that an absent capability is not a prohibition and a possible need no reason for complexity, stated once; the problem side sorts decided absences from unreached ones and states the cost of each. |
| R2, R3, R4 | B2, B3, C2, J2, J11 | Where the facts it needs can be read back, the problem's recursion can be derived; the design admits a runtime thing only at a real boundary; what recurses in the problem gets no tree in the runtime by itself. |
| R5, R6 | C4, J14, J17 | Moving the atomic case is one way of widening the foundation; the choice at a decision point is placed where the information is. |
| M1 The three readings | J7 The two readings of pressure; J2, J3, B5, J12 | M1 holds J7's two readings and adds the third: when the difficulty is the formulation's, the design side hands it to M1. A design reading found under M1 goes to the move that acts on it, not back to J7. |
| M2, M4 | D4, J9, J12, C4 | Reformulation operations and their record; the carrier rule and the premise lever are the design-side forms; C4 carries the migration of a live system. |
| L1 to L4 | D2, D6, "Working with others", the floor | Which loop the work is in, owned by L1; what each side hands the other; one floor, shared. Semantic consequence separates a change of what is asked (the holder of the meaning) from re-rendering and replacement (engineering, recorded under M4). |

Neither side comes first. A design session may open on the problem side because the problem is unclear, or on the design side because a system exists and strains; a construction difficulty read through J7 may turn out to be a formulation fault and move to M1; a reformulation may change a premise that removes several mechanisms through J12. What keeps the two from becoming two sets of principles is that a session, a brief or a round of work names which loop it is in (L1) and, when it switches, says why.

## Revising this Skill

The methodology is meant to change. It is itself a representation of how a set of formulations and design judgments were produced and revised, and its fit to the history it was drawn from does not show that it fits another problem or the whole of another system. That history and its cases are its evidence, not its authority, and the same holds for any one person's statement of it. For example, in that history the substantive revisions moved one way, and where a ruling went the other way its owner later reversed it, with a reason in the same direction stated in general terms (see [references/cases.md](references/cases.md)): the stable thing is the reasoning, and particular rulings are its applications. What the Skill can transmit is enough to continue the work from it: the principles, the moves, their questions and their reasons. The understanding its authors held while forming them is rebuilt by applying it, not by reading it.

What warrants a change. A principle or move should change when design or construction experience shows that following it produced a worse system or a worse formulation, or that it was silent on a decision that mattered, and the reason can be stated in general terms rather than as the preference of the case. A single surprising outcome is a case to record; a pattern across cases is a reason to revise.

How to change it. Apply the methodology to itself, as it revises a problem and a design. Read the difficulty three ways first: the Skill may lack a principle or move, one may be stated wrongly, or a question may have been put to one side of the Skill that belongs to the other. Revise at the premise: prefer sharpening a principle's or a move's statement or its "what it is not" to adding a new one for a new case, as a platform is widened rather than given a special case (C4, D4). Name the operation and its form, and write the record (M4), including what a removed or narrowed principle or move used to protect and what protects it now (J9); watch for it returning under another name. Rewrite the affected text in its current form, without history inside it (D5).

Where the record goes. Add the case that forced the change to [references/cases.md](references/cases.md), and add a row to [CHANGES.md](CHANGES.md) beside this file (what changed, the case or cases that forced it, what it replaces). A revision that touches a join (see "How the two sides join") is made on both sides of this Skill in one pass and recorded in the same row. If the change comes out of a project's own research or decision record, record it there as well and point to it. Do not amend old cases to fit the new formulation; a later lesson is recorded as a later case.
